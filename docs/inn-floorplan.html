The WhiskedAway Inn

A floorplan for the Inn as a place you can wander: which room is where, what each window looks out on, and what plays in every room. This is the map the ChatGPT briefs are drawn from.

The plan

The Inn sits on a headland above the sea. The back of the house faces the water, the front faces the village lane, the sunny side opens onto the garden and the glasshouse, and the shady side backs onto the hill. Click a room to jump to its notes.

Built (360° panorama) To build Locked on purpose Magic dial beside the door

Two ways through every door

Walk

Click the door and you step into the hallway. From there the Inn is a world you explore on foot: the hallway, the entrance hall, the stairs, the landing, every room's own door. This is what makes the Inn feel real.

Turn the dial

Beside every door is the same brass dial. Turn it to a room, open the door, and you're there, with a one-second glimpse of the room through the doorframe as it opens. This is the fixed navigation: always in the same place, always works.

Gemma's notch

One notch on the dial has a little star. Set it there and the door opens wherever your wander points: lingering on rainy-day comfort might open the reading corner of the library. This is the exploratory navigation, built into the furniture.

Room by room

Each room keeps the tome it already has, gets a view that suits it, and has one thing in it that plays. What plays changes with the barometer and the clock. Pieces are suggestions, all public-domain compositions (see the note on recordings below).

Making it feel bigger than it is

The visitor sees a handful of rooms but should believe in many more. These cost very little to build:

  • Locked doors that stay locked. The guest rooms are "full tonight". The attic is "not today, love". The cellar door under the stairs is stiff.
  • Light behind doors. A door left ajar with warm light and a sound behind it, before that room is built.
  • Sound through the walls. The grandfather clock, the phonograph two rooms away, a kettle somewhere.
  • Windows onto other parts of the Inn. From the window seat you see the glasshouse roof; at night the turret window is lit.
  • The Inn cat. A cat who is somewhere different depending on the weather and the time: on the glasshouse bench in the sun, by the range in the rain, in the turret at night. Following the cat is a wander of its own, and the navigator can notice it.
  • The notice board. In the entrance hall, with what's in season, tonight's soup and whatever Gemma has pinned up for the visitor.

How sound behaves

  • Nothing plays until the visitor clicks something. Browsers require it, and it's good manners.
  • The one sound switch in Gemma's chat window turns everything on or off, the uh-oh included.
  • Click the object to start or stop it: lift the music box lid, wind the phonograph, turn the radio dial.
  • Turning the barometer or pushing the clock changes the piece, with a short fade between them.
  • Starting a piece counts as part of the wander, so music taste can steer what Gemma serves.

A note on the recordings

These compositions are out of copyright, but every recording of them has its own rights. We'd use only recordings that are free to use: CC0 or public-domain performances from Musopen and Wikimedia Commons, or very old recordings that are public domain where we publish. The player piano is the neat case: scans of old piano rolls can be played back through a free piano sound, so it really is the roll playing. Every recording goes into the "Made with open things" credits.

Good guesses, never locks

The Inn guesses from where the visitor seems to be, then always lets them change it. The guess comes from the country and rough latitude Cloudflare attaches to each visit. It can be wrong (a VPN in another country, for one), which is fine, because it's only a starting point.

  • Season. The glasshouse opens in the visitor's season for their hemisphere: October is spring in Auckland and autumn in Edinburgh. The seasons wheel turns it to any season, for anyone who wants to learn about spring planting in autumn.
  • Time of day. Already works this way: the device's own clock, until the visitor pushes the hands.
  • Weather. Already works this way: the barometer starts on rain and Gemma asks what it's like for them.
  • Country for shopping lists. Grocery suggestions start with the visitor's country and its supermarkets, with a country picker on the list itself.

Only the guess is used, in the page. Nothing about location is stored or kept after the visit.

Decided

  1. The rooms stay where they are on the plan.
  2. The dial shows room names, plus Gemma's star notch for "wherever your wander points".
  3. The glasshouse opens in the visitor's real season, and they can turn it to any other.
  4. Build order: the hallway and the magic door, then the glasshouse, then Flourish. ChatGPT also draws the outside of the Inn as a storybook illustration, held inside a whisk the way a genie is held in a lamp.
